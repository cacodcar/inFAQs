The renewable input limits are 100 each. Conversion efficiencies of 0.75 for PV and 0.85 for wind give electricity limits of 75 and 85.

Oxygen leaves as a byproduct, without revenue or a demand requirement. The model covers one period, with fixed supply limits and no storage. Nonnegative solar and wind dispatch also make the remaining resource flows nonnegative through the balances.

Why does the decision change?

Solar supplies the electrolyzer first. At a hydrogen demand of 1.425, solar reaches its limit and wind covers the additional electricity. Below the diagonal water boundary, neither energy source can make the request feasible.

About this example

This single-period model comes from Energia’s parametric hydrogen notebook. Its illustrative coefficients and parameter ranges are preserved; physical units are unspecified in the notebook, so values use original study units.

The related paper Robust Scheduling of Energy Systems Under Forecasting Uncertainty considers a different network: wind generation, a small modular nuclear reactor, and lithium-ion storage over 24 periods. Its parameter controls the size of the wind-forecast uncertainty set. Here, d and w specify individual demand and water scenarios; this explorer does not compute robust schedules.

Computed with Energia, Gana, and PPOPT; checked against independent direct linear optimization. Values and rules are rounded for display. Download the solution · Validation record
