Why does the decision change?

Solar supplies the electrolyzer first. At a hydrogen demand of 1.425, solar reaches its limit and wind covers the additional electricity. Below the diagonal water boundary, neither energy source can make the request feasible.

From Energia’s hydrogen-production model

This is the existing single-period parametric hydrogen case study, with its demand range, water range, conversion coefficients, and illustrative costs preserved. Quantities and costs use the notebook’s original study units.

minimize 567 S + 990 V + 0.001 U

subject to H = 0.019 (S + V) ≥ D

U = 0.1753 (S + V) ≤ W

0 ≤ S ≤ 75, 0 ≤ V ≤ 85

parameters 0.5 ≤ D ≤ 2.5, 6 ≤ W ≤ 28

S and V are solar and wind electricity, H is delivered hydrogen, and U is water consumed. The electricity balance eliminates the electrolyzer’s power input. Solar and wind output limits follow from the original input limits of 100 and conversion efficiencies of 0.75 and 0.85.

Water required is U = (0.1753 / 0.019) D. More water than this gives spare capacity; less makes the request infeasible. This is a single-period operating model with fixed renewable supply limits.

The case study uses illustrative coefficients. Its two feasible critical regions describe solar-only operation and combined solar–wind operation.
